ChatGPT prompts for accountants, without risking client data

The most useful ChatGPT prompts for accountants are for drafting: client document requests, chasers, plain-English explanations, checklists and fee or scope emails. Write them with placeholders such as [client name] instead of real client details, and fill those in yourself afterwards. Before using ChatGPT for work, check your firm's policy and your account's data settings, because individual accounts may be used for model training unless you turn that off.

These ChatGPT prompts for accountants are for the writing that surrounds the real work: emails, checklists and explanations. Used carefully, AI chat tools save time there. The rule that matters most is simple: the prompt shouldn't contain anything you couldn't put on a postcard.

Below you'll find the rules to follow first, the data settings to check, eight prompts you can copy, and the point where copying prompts stops being worth it.

Rules before any prompt

  • No client names, addresses, tax references, Social Security numbers, bank details or figures in a general AI chat
  • Use placeholders like [client name] and fill them in yourself afterwards, outside the chat
  • Check your firm's policy and the AI tool's data settings before using it for work
  • Read every draft. You're responsible for anything sent in your name
  • Never ask it for advice on a specific client's tax position

Check your ChatGPT data settings first

OpenAI's help pages (checked November 2026) explain how this works:

  • On individual ChatGPT accounts, OpenAI may use your content to train its models unless you opt out. The setting is called "Improve the model for everyone", in ChatGPT's data controls. Turning it off keeps your chat history.
  • A Temporary Chat stays out of your history and isn't used to train models while it stays temporary, but OpenAI may keep a copy for up to 30 days for safety purposes.
  • OpenAI says it doesn't use content from its business workspaces (ChatGPT Business and Enterprise) or from the API to train models by default. Your organisation's own retention and access settings still apply.

The rules behind the settings

In the UK, UK GDPR keeps your firm accountable for client personal data it decides to process, whichever tool it uses (Article 5(2)). In the US, tax return preparers also have the IRS's section 7216 rules on using and disclosing tax return information, which can require the taxpayer's consent. Placeholders keep both questions out of your prompts entirely, which is why they're rule one.

Where prompts stop and automation starts

Copying drafts between a chat window and your email still takes time, and it's easy to slip client details into the chat by mistake. A workflow can use the same kind of drafting inside your own systems, fill in client details itself from your records, and put every draft in a queue for a person to approve before it's sent. Common questions

Is it safe to paste client documents into ChatGPT?

Not into a general chat. Keep client data in tools your firm has approved for it, with data settings you've checked. On individual ChatGPT accounts, content may be used for training unless you turn that off.

Does ChatGPT train on what accountants type into it?

OpenAI says it may use content from individual accounts for training unless you turn off "Improve the model for everyone", and that it doesn't train on business workspaces or the API by default.

Can AI give tax advice to clients?

It shouldn't. Use it for drafting and explaining general ideas. Advice stays with a qualified person.
