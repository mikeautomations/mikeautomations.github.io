When to start chasing Self Assessment clients: a dated timeline

Start chasing Self Assessment clients in the summer: send the first records request in June or July, once employers have issued P60s (due by 31 May) and P11D information (due by 6 July). Set a records cut-off in the autumn, well before Christmas, so you have time to prepare, review and file before HMRC's 31 January online filing and payment deadline. For the 2025 to 2026 tax year, that deadline is 31 January 2027.

By Michael Gershman · Published 10 November 2026 · 7 min read · UK

When to start chasing Self Assessment clients is really a question about your capacity, not HMRC's calendar. The online deadline is 31 January, but your deadline is the last date you can receive records and still prepare, review, get approval and file without the whole team working through the holidays.

This post gives a month-by-month timeline from the end of the tax year to 31 January, what to send at each point, how to set a records cut-off from your own numbers, and what to do with records that arrive after 1 January. The HMRC dates are from GOV.UK, checked in November 2026, for returns covering 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026.

The HMRC dates you are working back from

GOV.UK sets these dates for the 2025 to 2026 tax year. Everything else in your plan should sit in front of them, with room to spare.

Month by month: what to send and when

This is a suggested plan, not an HMRC rule. Move the dates to suit your practice, but keep the order: request early, remind on a rhythm, set a cut-off, and save January for review and filing.

How to set your records cut-off from your own numbers

A cut-off that isn't based on capacity is just a hope. Work it out backwards from 31 January in four steps.

  1. Count your Self Assessment returns, and split them into simple and complex (several income sources, gains, overseas income).
  2. Estimate how many returns your team can prepare and review in a normal week, then halve it for the weeks around Christmas.
  3. Leave at least two weeks at the end of January for client approval, filing and payment questions.
  4. Count back from that point by the number of weeks of preparation you need. The date you land on is the latest your final records can arrive. Set your published cut-off a few weeks earlier, so late clients still land before it.

For illustration: working out one practice's cut-off

For illustration: a two-person practice has 150 Self Assessment returns. Together they can prepare and review about 25 a week, or about 12 a week over the Christmas fortnight. They keep the last two weeks of January for approvals, so preparation must finish by mid-January.

If every client sent records on the same day, they would need about seven weeks of preparation, which means all records in by late November. In practice records arrive spread out from the summer, so most returns are done before the autumn. They publish a cut-off of 31 October and expect the final batch to arrive by the end of November. Anything later goes into a January slot with the late-records fee in their engagement letter.

What to do with records that arrive after 1 January

Late records will happen. Decide in advance how you handle them, so nobody makes it up under pressure.

  • Triage by size: a client missing one small figure goes ahead now; a client missing a whole income source waits for it.
  • Say clearly, in writing, what you can and can't promise for 31 January.
  • GOV.UK allows provisional figures when a figure genuinely isn't known by the deadline (for example while waiting for a valuation). The return must say so, it must be amended later, and interest runs on any extra tax from the original due date.
  • Make sure the client knows the payment is due on 31 January even if the return is late.
  • Apply any late-records fee only if your engagement letter sets it out.

What a missed 31 January costs your client

Put the real cost in your reminders. It does more than "please send soon". For 2025 to 2026 returns, GOV.UK lists these late filing penalties: £100 straight away, then £10 a day after 3 months (up to £900), then 5% of the tax due or £300 (whichever is greater) after 6 months, and the same again after 12 months. Late payment brings penalties of 5% of the tax unpaid at 30 days, 6 months and 12 months, plus interest.

Clients who joined Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from 6 April 2026 have new penalty rules from that tax year onwards, but GOV.UK confirms the current penalties still apply to their 2025 to 2026 return due on 31 January 2027. Those clients also have a quarterly update due by 7 February 2027, a week after the return, so ask for their records early.

Already November? A catch-up plan

If you're reading this in November and haven't sent requests, you can still get most of the season back. This week, send every client who hasn't sent records their own list, with a cut-off at the end of November and one upload link. Use our Self Assessment records checklist for clients to build each list. Then remind every 7 to 10 days with only what's still missing, and move to phone calls from 2 January.

Common questions

When should an accountant start chasing Self Assessment records?

Send the first full request in June or July, once employers have issued P60s (by 31 May) and P11D information (by 6 July). Remind on a steady rhythm through the autumn, and set a records cut-off well before Christmas.

What's a sensible records cut-off date?

Work it out from your capacity: count returns, divide by what your team can prepare each week, leave two weeks at the end of January for approvals, and count back. Run those numbers before you publish a date.

Is mid-December too late to start chasing?

For most practices, yes. December has fewer working days, clients are busy, and anything that arrives after Christmas competes with every other late file. Start in the summer, and use December for review and approvals.
