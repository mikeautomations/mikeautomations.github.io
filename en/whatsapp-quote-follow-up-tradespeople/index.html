WhatsApp quote follow-up for tradespeople: what to send and when

WhatsApp quote follow-up for tradespeople works best as three short messages: a check that the quote arrived and made sense, a useful nudge about five days later, and a polite message about a week after that which closes the quote, then you stop. If the messages go out automatically through the WhatsApp Business Platform, Meta only allows approved templates once 24 hours have passed since the customer last wrote, and the customer must have agreed to hear from your business by name.

WhatsApp quote follow-up for tradespeople is the job most UK trades never get round to. You price a rewire, a bathroom or a boiler swap, send it on WhatsApp, see the two blue ticks, and then hear nothing. Chasing feels pushy, so it doesn't happen, and the job goes to whoever asked again. This page gives you a three-message sequence with timing, explains how WhatsApp Business templates and opt-in work under Meta's own rules, and splits what can run on its own from what should stay with you.

Why customers go quiet after a quote

Only the last one is a lost job. The others are a reply that never got written, and a short, friendly message usually brings it out. A clear "no" is useful too: you can stop holding the date and stop thinking about it. The same short sequence works outside the trades, for example a caterer following up an event quote. Painters and decorators can run the same sequence with a quote and colour sign-off workflow that also records every colour and finish before the first coat.

  • They're waiting on other quotes and haven't decided yet
  • They read it on the bus, meant to reply, and it slid down the chat list
  • A line in the quote confused them (what's included, VAT, the start date) and they didn't want to ask
  • The price was higher than they hoped, and silence feels easier than saying so
  • They've picked someone else and don't want the awkward conversation

How it runs, step by step

  1. You send the quote as usual. The workflow logs the customer, the amount and the date.
  2. A day later the customer gets a short WhatsApp message checking that the quote arrived and asking if anything needs explaining.
  3. About five days after that, a second message adds something useful: your next available dates, or what happens first on the job.
  4. About a week later, a third message politely closes the quote and asks whether to keep the slot open.
  5. Any reply, a booking or a deposit stops the sequence at once, and the conversation carries on with you, by hand.
  6. You see the open quotes and their last message on one list, and call the ones worth a call.

A 3-message WhatsApp follow-up sequence

This timing is a starting point, not a rule. Shorten it for urgent work like a leak or a failed boiler, and stretch it for a kitchen or an extension that the customer will plan for weeks. How WhatsApp Business templates and opt-in work

If you send these yourself from the free WhatsApp Business app, you're simply replying to a customer who contacted you. The app's own tools help (saved quick replies, greeting and away messages), but you still press send. To send a timed sequence automatically, the messages go through the WhatsApp Business Platform (Meta's Cloud API), usually via a provider, and Meta's developer documentation sets the rules (we compare the free app and the Platform in more detail):

  • When a customer messages you, a 24-hour customer service window opens, and each new message from them resets it. Inside the window you can send normal messages
  • Outside that window you can only send message templates that Meta has reviewed and approved. Meta says review can take up to 24 hours. Templates use placeholders such as {{1}} for the customer's name and the job
  • Every template has a category: utility, marketing or authentication. Meta describes utility templates as non-promotional and tied to something the customer asked for. Add a discount or an offer and it becomes marketing, which Meta prices differently
  • Opt-in: Meta requires that the customer clearly agreed to receive messages from your business, with your business name stated. A line on your quote such as "We'll follow up about this quote on WhatsApp" is a simple way to cover it
  • Give people a clear way to stop, and honour it. Meta recommends this, and it's good manners anyway

A note on UK marketing rules

The ICO's guidance on direct marketing treats purely administrative or customer service messages differently from marketing, but says a message with any promotional element counts as direct marketing. A follow-up about the customer's own quote, with no offers, sits on the service side. A message offering a discount or advertising other work does not. If you're unsure about your wording, read the ICO's guidance or ask an adviser.

Common questions

How many times should I chase a quote?

Three messages over about two weeks is a sensible default for most domestic jobs. After the third, stop. A customer who wants the job will come back, and one who's been chased five times usually won't.

Should I follow up on WhatsApp or by text?

Use the channel the customer used to contact you. If they came in on WhatsApp, follow up there. If they phoned or emailed, a text or an email may feel less intrusive. The same sequence works on any of them.

Is the WhatsApp Business app enough, or do I need the Platform?

If you're happy to press send yourself, the free app with saved quick replies is enough. Automatic, timed messages need the Platform, approved templates and opt-in. On the call we check which suits the number of quotes you send.
